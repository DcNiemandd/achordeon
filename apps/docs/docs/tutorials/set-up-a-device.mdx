# Set up a device

Install it, then make sure the library is not only in this browser.

Achordeon has no server holding your songs. The library is a database inside the
browser you are reading this in — **IndexedDB**, on this machine, under this
address — and that is what makes the app fast, private and usable on a train.
It is also the whole risk: a library that exists in exactly one place exists
until something clears it.

So this page is two jobs. The first three steps make the app a proper app on
this device. The seven after them make the library recoverable from somewhere
that is not this device — by hand into a file, into your own Google Drive, or
continuously between machines. Do the first three today; do at least one of the
others before you have written anything you would mind retyping.

## 1. Open the app in the browser

Achordeon lives at `achordeon.eu/app` — <AppLink>open it now</AppLink>. There is
nothing to install first, no account, and no empty screen at the end of it: the
first visit writes a starter library, so every module opens on real songs and a
real songbook instead of on its own blank state. They are ordinary songs from
that moment — rename them, rewrite them, throw them away.

Bookmark the address. It is the only one there is, and it is the same address
whether you end up running the app in a tab or in a window of its own.

That first visit also creates the database. Two things follow from where it
lives, and both matter more than they sound:

- **A browser is a device.** Chrome and Firefox on the same laptop are two
  Achordeons with two libraries that know nothing about each other. So is a
  private window, which throws its library away when you close it.
- **Nobody else has a copy.** Not us — no library reaches a server unless you
  switch on the sync in step 9.

:::warning[Clearing site data clears the library]

The browser's own "clear cookies and site data" takes IndexedDB with it, and it
is offered next to the things people clear routinely. Uninstalling the app or
resetting the browser does the same. Nothing on this page stops that happening —
steps 6 to 9 are what make it survivable.

:::

## 2. Install it

Achordeon is a Progressive Web App, which means the browser can promote it from
a page to something that behaves like an installed program. There is no Install
button anywhere in Achordeon, because the control is not ours: your browser owns
it, and where it sits depends on which browser you are holding.

- **Chrome, Edge or Brave on a desktop** — an install icon at the right-hand end
  of the address bar, or the same entry in the `⋮` menu.
- **Chrome on Android** — `⋮`, then _Install app_ or _Add to Home screen_.
- **Safari on iPhone or iPad** — the Share button, then _Add to Home Screen_.
  There is no other way in on iOS, and it is easy to walk past.
- **Firefox on a desktop** does not install web apps at all. Everything on this
  page still works; it stays a tab.

What you get for it is a window with no address bar and no tab strip, an icon in
the launcher or on the home screen, and — on a Chromium desktop — a file
association: double-click an `.achordeon` file and it opens in the app.

What you do **not** get for it is offline, because you already had it. The
service worker that keeps a copy of the app itself is registered on your first
visit whether you install or not; installing changes the frame around the app,
not what it can do without a connection.

:::note[Installing does not make a second library]

The installed window and the tab are the same address, so they read the same
database. Install after a month of writing and everything is already there;
uninstall and it is still in the tab. What you install is the app, never a copy
of your songs.

:::

An installed app has no app store behind it, so updating is Achordeon's own job.
It checks when it starts and again whenever you come back to the window, and a
new version that has finished downloading announces itself as a bar with two
buttons: <Ui id="update.reload" /> takes it now, <Ui id="update.later" /> puts it
off. It never reloads on its own — you could be halfway through a song in front
of a room. The bar comes back on the next check, which is as insistent as a
routine deploy gets.

Two updates do insist, and both say why. <Ui id="update.required.title" /> means
something handed the app data written by a newer version, which it will not guess
at. <Ui id="update.broken.title" /> means the offline cache is damaged beyond
repair — and it is careful to tell you that _your songs are stored separately and
are not affected_, because a reload prompt after months of writing deserves that
sentence.

## 3. Set the theme and the language

Everything on this step is in <AppLink to="settings">Settings</AppLink>, in the
section headed <Ui id="settings.app" />. Scroll past the account block at the top
— that is steps 5 to 9 — and this is the next card down.

  src="/img/screenshot-settings-app.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-settings-app-dark.png"
  shadow
/>

<Ui id="settings.theme" /> is three buttons. <Ui id="theme.light" /> and
<Ui id="theme.dark" /> are decisions; <Ui id="theme.system" /> hands the
decision to the machine, and follows it live — a laptop that goes dark at dusk
takes the app with it without a reload.

The checkbox beside it, <Ui id="settings.songDark.on" />, is a different
question: it turns over the **paper** rather than the app. With it on, every song
Achordeon draws goes light-on-dark while the app is dark — previews, the editor's
render, a performance. Printing, PDFs and downloads stay on white whatever it
says, and so does anybody watching you from their own phone. There is more of it
in [Perform from a songbook](./perform-from-a-songbook.mdx#7-turn-the-page-dark).

<Ui id="settings.language" /> offers English and Čeština, each written in its
own language rather than in the one you are currently stuck in — which is the
point, since somebody hunting for Czech in an English app is looking for the
word _Čeština_. Choosing one **reloads the app**, and the hint beside the row
says so: _Achordeon reloads to switch language, and stays on the page you are
on._ A message is translated once, on first use, so a running app cannot be
re-lettered in place.

:::note[This section belongs to the device, not to the account]

The theme, the dark page, the language, the panel sizing and the statistics
switch are all stored on the machine you set them on. They do not travel — not in
a backup, not over sync — so a second device is three clicks of setting up, and a
phone can run dark while the desktop runs light. Everything under <Ui id="settings.rendering" /> below is the opposite: those are your account's
defaults, and they do travel.

:::

## 4. Use it with no connection

Turn the aeroplane on and reload. The app comes up.

Two separate things make that true, and knowing which is which explains
everything that follows. The **app** — the code, the four bundled fonts, both
translation files — was copied onto the device by the service worker on your
first visit, and is served from there rather than from the network. The
**library** was never anywhere else to begin with.

So the whole of the songwriting side works with nothing connected: writing and
editing, the live render, transposing, building and reordering songbooks,
printing, PDFs, PNGs and ZIPs, performing a set on stage, importing a file
somebody dropped on the window, and even opening a share link — the song is
inside the link, so there is nothing to fetch. Backing up to a file and restoring
from one work too, which is exactly why they are the steps that need no account.

Two things are worth doing while you still have a connection:

- **Open a song in each title font once.** Achordeon ships four faces, and only
  the one the app is written in is copied down at install time; the other three
  are fetched the first time a page actually needs one. A font you have never
  rendered is a font that may not be there in the dark.
- **Add the fonts you want before you go.** Adding one downloads it (step 10) —
  but once added it is kept on the device and never fetched again. See
  [Add a font](./add-a-font.mdx).

Step 10 is the list of what genuinely stops. It is shorter than people expect.

## 5. Sign in

Everything up to here needed no account, and that is not an accident: the account
buys you exactly one thing, which is getting the library off this single device.

Three things sit behind it, and they are not all the same price:

- **Google Drive backup** — free, and step 7.
- **Automatic sync** — <Ui id="settings.account.pro" />, and step 9.
- **Hosting an audience lobby** — <Ui id="settings.account.pro" />, though it is
  lent to free accounts while it is being tested. See
  [Play to an audience](./play-to-an-audience.mdx).

The account block is the first card on <AppLink to="settings">Settings</AppLink>,
and offers two doors into the same account. <Ui id="settings.account.google" />
is one tap and also connects the identity Drive rides on. <Ui id="settings.account.login" /> opens this:

<Image
  alt="The Log in dialog: email, password, and a link for a password you have forgotten"
  src="/img/screenshot-login-dialog.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-login-dialog-dark.png"
  shadow
/>

If you have not got an account yet, <Ui id="settings.account.register" /> beside
it takes the same two fields plus a confirmation of the password, and then sends
you an email: the method is not live until you click the link in it. A password
you have lost is <Ui id="settings.account.forgotLink" /> in the dialog above,
which mails you a link to set a new one.

You are not choosing between the two doors for good. Once you are in, the account
grows the method it is missing — <Ui id="settings.account.linkGoogle" /> or <Ui id="settings.account.addPassword" /> — and after that either one gets you in.
Signing in with Google a second time never merges you into somebody else's
account; it attaches to yours.

:::note[Signing in with Google is not granting Drive]

The sign-in asks for your email address and basic profile and nothing else.
Permission to write a file in your Drive is a separate grant, asked the first
time you press a Drive button in step 7 — and it covers only the file Achordeon
itself creates. See
[your account & Google data](/settings-account/account-data.mdx#google-drive).

:::

One thing to know before you press it, because it surprises people. **The first
sign-in claims the library on this device.** From then on those songs belong to
that account, and Achordeon shows them only to it: sign out and the lists go
empty — the songs are not deleted, they are hidden, and signing back in brings
them straight back. Sign in as somebody else in the same browser and you get told
so in as many words — <Ui id="library.foreign.heading" /> — with the choice of
signing in as the owner instead, or <Ui id="library.foreign.replace" />, which
clears this device's copy of their library and puts yours here. One browser holds
one library, and it belongs to one account.

## 6. Back up to a file

The one on this page that needs no account, no connection and nobody's
permission. It is in the <Ui id="settings.sync" /> block of the account card,
under <Ui id="settings.backup" />: press <Ui id="settings.backup.save" /> and a
file lands, named `achordeon-backup-` and today's date.

What is in it is worth being exact about, because it is not what an export is.
A backup is the **entire database, verbatim** — every song and every songbook,
your global render defaults, the record of the songs you have deleted, and the
font files you added yourself, which no other file in Achordeon carries. It is
not a selection and there is nothing to choose: the button is the whole act.

Sending a few songs to somebody is the other job, and the other file. That is
[Share and import songs](./share-a-song.mdx), and the app says so in the same
block: _Sending a few songs to someone else is a different job._

Where the file goes is your browser's business rather than the app's — Chromium
asks, Firefox and Safari drop it in the downloads folder. Put it somewhere that
is not this device. A backup sitting in the Downloads folder of the machine you
are protecting against is a backup of nothing.

:::tip[The cheapest habit on this page]

Press it after an evening of writing, keep the last few files, delete the rest.
It costs two seconds and it is the only step here that works on a machine that
has never been signed into anything.

:::

## 7. Grant Drive access and back up there

The same backup, kept in your own Google Drive instead of in a file you have to
remember to move. <Ui id="settings.drive.heading" /> is the row under <Ui id="settings.backup" />, with <Ui id="settings.drive.upload" /> and <Ui id="settings.drive.download" /> on it. It is free — Premium starts at step 9.

Both buttons are dead until Google is attached to your account, and the line
under them names the step that is actually missing rather than the one you have
already done: <Ui id="settings.drive.requiresSignIn" /> while you are signed out,
and <Ui id="settings.drive.requires" /> once you are in by email. Go back to step
5 for that.

The first <Ui id="settings.drive.upload" /> is where Google asks. The permission
is the narrow one: Achordeon can see and touch **only the backup file it wrote
itself**, and the rest of your Drive stays invisible to it — it cannot list your
files, read them, or know they are there.

Uploading **merges with the copy already in Drive** rather than flattening it, so
a backup pushed from your laptop does not throw away what your phone put there
this morning. When the Drive copy has moved on further than Achordeon expected it
says so — <Ui id="settings.drive.conflict" /> — and offers <Ui id="settings.drive.force" />, which is the one you press when you know your
copy is the one that should win.

Coming the other way, <Ui id="settings.drive.download" /> asks the same question a
file does, in the same dialog, with the same two answers. That is step 8.

## 8. Bring a backup back: add to the library, or replace everything

<Ui id="settings.backup.restore" /> opens the file picker; the Drive button
above fetches the copy from there. Either way you land here, because a backup is
a backup wherever it was kept:

<Image
  alt="The restore dialog: what Add and Replace each do, then Cancel, Replace everything and Add to my library"
  src="/img/screenshot-restore-dialog.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-restore-dialog-dark.png"
  shadow
/>

Nothing has been written yet. The dialog describes both answers in full before
either can be pressed, because only the person holding the file knows which of
two completely different jobs it is here to do.

**<Ui id="settings.restore.merge" />** brings the backup's songs and songbooks in
beside the ones you have. Where the same song is in both, the newer copy wins,
one row at a time — the same rule import, Drive and sync all reconcile by. Your
settings are not touched, and neither is anything the backup has never heard of.
Deletions ride along like any other change, so an old backup does not resurrect
the songs you threw away last month.

**<Ui id="settings.restore.replace" />** puts the library back exactly as the
backup has it — settings, added fonts, the lot — and **anything not in the backup
is gone**. That is the one irreversible button on this page. It is the right
answer to "put this machine back the way it was" and the wrong answer to almost
everything else.

Either way the app reloads when it is done, because the version of your library
it was holding a moment ago no longer exists.

A file that is not an Achordeon backup, or one that arrived damaged, gets <Ui id="settings.restore.failedTitle" /> and the reassurance that goes with it:
_Your library is unchanged._ Nothing is half-applied.

:::tip[Add is the one you usually want]

<Ui id="settings.restore.replace" /> is for a machine being rebuilt. If you are
after a song out of an old backup, <Ui id="settings.restore.merge" /> gets it
without costing you anything you have written since.

:::

## 9. Switch on automatic sync

<Ui id="settings.autoSync" />, at the top of the <Ui id="settings.sync" />
block, is the step that stops this being a chore. Tick
<Ui id="settings.autoSync.on" /> and the library keeps itself the same on every
device you sign in on, with no file in the story at all.

It is <Ui id="settings.account.pro" />, and it is the one Premium feature that is
genuinely held back rather than lent for testing: on a free or signed-out account
the checkbox is dead and says why — <Ui id="settings.autoSync.requires" /> The Premium badge on it disappears once
you have paid, since marking somebody's own feature as an upsell is just noise.

It is not, despite what people expect of the word, keystroke-by-keystroke. It
works on **boundaries**: a song saved, a book reordered, a global default changed
— each schedules a push a few seconds later, so a burst of edits costs one round
trip rather than thirty. It pulls when the app launches and every time you come
back to the window, which is the moment a change made on the other device
matters, and it flushes whatever is still pending when you leave. Two devices
edited apart reconcile row by row, newest wins, exactly as a restore does.

The row tells you where it has got to rather than leaving you guessing: <Ui id="settings.sync.syncing" /> while it runs, <Ui id="settings.sync.unsynced" /> when there is something still to go, <Ui id="settings.sync.synced" /> when there is not, and <Ui id="settings.sync.failed" /> when a round trip did not land — which is a
statement of fact rather than a request, since it tries again by itself. Close
the tab with work that has not gone up and the browser asks whether you meant to.

While it is running it takes the manual tools out of your hands, and both rows
say so: <Ui id="settings.backup.blocked" /> The cloud is already the copy, and a
restore by hand would be pushed straight back up over every other device.

:::note[Sync is not a backup]

Sync's job is to make every device agree, and it does that faithfully in both
directions: delete a song on the phone and it is gone from the laptop within
seconds. A backup is the thing that still has it afterwards. They answer
different questions, and a device that matters is worth both.

:::

## 10. Know what still needs a connection

Everything Achordeon does with your songs works offline. What needs the network
is, without exception, something that involves somebody else's computer:

- **Signing in, registering, and resetting a password.** The session already on
  the device keeps working; getting a new one does not.
- **Automatic sync.** It queues and catches up when you are back; the row reads <Ui id="settings.sync.unsynced" /> in the meantime.
- **Google Drive**, in both directions.
- **Adding a font.** Searching Google Fonts and fetching the file both go out.
  Once a font is on the device it is never fetched again — including in a
  performance, and including in a PDF.
- **Hosting or joining an audience lobby.** Every phone in the room is talking to
  the same server, so this one is online by definition. Performing from the stage
  yourself is not.
- **Reporting a bug** from <Ui id="settings.about.heading" />, and the <Ui id="settings.about.docsButton" /> and <Ui id="settings.about.patchNotesButton" /> buttons beside it, which open this
  site.
- **Checking for a new version.** It fails quietly: not knowing about an update
  breaks nothing.

Nothing else. Not the editor, not the renderer, not a PDF, not a songbook, not a
performance, not an import, not a backup to a file and not a restore from one.
That is the point of the whole arrangement — and it is why the seven steps above
are worth doing before the evening you need them.
