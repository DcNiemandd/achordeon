# Play to an audience

Your songbook on their phones, turning when you turn.

A lobby is five characters. You open one from inside a performance, everybody
else types those five characters into the <Ui id="nav.audience" /> module, and
from then on their screens show the song yours is on.

What travels is the **song**, not a picture of your screen: the words, the
chords and the settings the page is drawn with, so every phone lays it out for
its own screen instead of squinting at a shrunk copy of yours. Hosting needs a
connection; joining is free, and always will be.

## 1. As the performer: enter a performance

There is no lobby outside one. Open <AppLink to="stage">Stage</AppLink>, pick
the songbook you are playing tonight, and you are performing —
[Perform from a songbook](./perform-from-a-songbook.mdx) covers that part if it
is new. Everything below happens on top of that page.

The lobby belongs to the **performance**, not to the songbook: it starts when
you open it and it is over when the performance is.

## 2. Create an audience

The audience control is in the performing bar with the others. On a desktop it
is the icon whose tooltip reads <Ui id="stage.audience" />; on a phone it is a
row of the same name inside <Ui id="stage.menu" />.

It opens a dialog that so far has nothing to show — one sentence about what
sharing does, and one button, <Ui id="stage.audienceDialog.create" />.

<Image
  alt="The audience dialog before a lobby exists: what sharing does, and the Create lobby button"
  src="/img/screenshot-stage-audience-create.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-stage-audience-create-dark.png"
/>

The gold ring around the button is the Premium mark, and hovering it says so:
hosting an audience is a Premium feature, lent to everyone free while the app is
in testing. The button is not disabled and never has been — press it.

Pressing it hands you a PIN straight away. The five characters are drawn on your
own device, from an alphabet with no `0`/`O` and no `1`/`I`/`L` in it, because
this is a code somebody reads off a screen across a room or hears called out.

## 3. Show the PIN, the link or the QR code

The dialog is now the lobby, and it offers the same room three ways.

<Image
  alt="The live lobby: the PIN, the count of listeners, the join link and the QR code"
  src="/img/screenshot-stage-lobby.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-stage-lobby-dark.png"
/>

- The code itself, under <Ui id="stage.audienceDialog.pin" /> — big enough to be
  read from the back, short enough to say out loud between songs.
- The address, under <Ui id="stage.audienceDialog.link" />. Click it and it is
  copied; the clipboard mark turns into a tick for a moment to say it worked.
  This is the one to paste into whatever the room is already looking at — a chat,
  an invitation, a description under a video.
- The same address as a picture, under <Ui id="stage.audienceDialog.qr" />. Hold
  the screen up, or put it on the projector between numbers, and a camera does
  the typing.

The row marked <Ui id="stage.audienceDialog.count" /> is how many devices are on
the lobby right now. It is the only honest answer to "did that work" — watch it
go up as the room joins.

:::note[Don't follow your own link]
The link opens the audience page, and opening it on the performing device would
be the stage walking off itself. Hand it over, don't click it.
:::

## 4. Play — the page turns for them

Nothing else to press. Every move you make to another song republishes the
lobby, and the viewers redraw. So does a change of settings mid-set: the page
they get is always the page you are looking at.

They also get the **set list** with it, which is why a viewer can open the songs
of the evening without asking you for anything.

Three things you do on stage stay on stage, on purpose:

- your own transpose — every viewer has one of their own, and one sent from the
  stage would fight it;
- the dark page (<Ui id="stage.darkPage" />) — a stage is dark and a kitchen
  table is not, so each screen answers for the room it is in;
- the zoom — leaning in is a thing you do with your own eyes.

## 5. Manage the lobby

Closing the dialog does not close the lobby. The audience control stays lit and
renames itself <Ui id="stage.audience.manage" />; opening it again puts you back
on the same PIN, the same link and the same count.

The lobby also survives leaving the page. Step out to <AppLink to="songs">Songs</AppLink> to fix a typo, or
reload the tab your phone closed while it was locked, and the performance — the
lobby included — resumes where it was. A performance you have not touched for
twelve hours does not.

## 6. End it by exiting

There is an <Ui id="stage.audienceDialog.end" /> at the foot of the dialog: it
retires the lobby and leaves you performing. <Ui id="stage.exit" /> does both at
once — the performance is over, so the audience is too.

Either way, the viewers are told: their screens read <Ui id="audience.ended" />
and offer the PIN prompt again.

A lobby is one evening. The next one is a new PIN, so a code somebody wrote down
last week gets them nowhere.

## 7. As a viewer: open `Audience`

This half needs nothing: no account, no library, no songs of your own. The
module is the fourth in the rail, under <Ui id="nav.stage" /> — <AppLink to="audience">open it now</AppLink>.

## 8. Type the PIN, or scan the QR code

<Image
  alt="The Audience module's join prompt: the PIN field and the Join button"
  src="/img/screenshot-audience-join.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-audience-join-dark.png"
/>

Type the five characters and press <Ui id="audience.join" />. Case does not
matter — the field puts everything in capitals as you go.

Scanning the performer's QR code skips this screen: the code carries the PIN in
the address, so the camera opens the app already joined. So does the link, which
is why the link is worth pasting where the room can reach it.

If the PIN is wrong you are told so — <Ui id="audience.notFound" /> — and left
here to try again, which is the point of an alphabet that cannot be misread. The
last PIN that actually worked is filled in for you next time, so rejoining after
a break is one tap.

## 9. Follow along, with the summary and the zoom

You are in. The song fills the screen and turns when the performer turns it.

<Image
  alt="A phone following a performance, with the audience controls in the bottom bar"
  src="/img/screenshot-audience-following.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-audience-following-dark.png"
/>

The bar at the foot is yours: fullscreen, the song list, and a <Ui id="stage.menu" /> for
the rest. On a desktop it sits under the page instead of at the bottom of the
screen, but it is the same three.

The song list is the set list the performer sent, marked at the song being
played. It has its own search and it is read-only — you are following, so
tapping a row does not take you anywhere.

<Image
  alt="The audience's song list: the set list, read-only, with the current song marked"
  src="/img/screenshot-audience-summary.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-audience-summary-dark.png"
/>

Everything else about how you read it is yours alone and invisible to the
performer:

- Zoom — pinch, or double-tap the line you want; `+`, `-` and `0` do it from a
  keyboard. A pill in the corner shows how far in you are and gives the whole
  page back in one tap.
- Chords off (<Ui id="audience.hideChords" />), for anyone in the room who is
  singing rather than playing. The chord rows keep their space, so nothing
  reflows under your eyes.
- The dark page (<Ui id="stage.darkPage" />), for the same song on black.
- Holding the phone sideways (<Ui id="stage.turnPage" />). The row is only there
  when the song on screen would gain by it, and armed, it turns the songs that
  do and leaves the rest upright.
- The lobby itself (<Ui id="audience.lobby" />) — the PIN, the count, and a QR
  code of your own. That is how you get the person beside you onto the same
  lobby without going back to the performer.

Last in the menu is <Ui id="audience.exit" />. The performance carries on
without you, and the PIN is remembered for the way back.

## 10. Transpose your own screen

The performer is playing it in the key it is written in. You have a capo on, or
a different instrument, or a voice that does not reach the top note.

The menu's <Ui id="transpose.title" /> opens a small panel over the page: down,
the offset, up. It stays open while you step, so you can watch the chords move
under it and stop where your hands are.

<Image
  alt="A viewer's own transpose: the panel over the page, and the chords already moved"
  src="/img/screenshot-audience-transpose.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-audience-transpose-dark.png"
/>

The number is semitones, signed, and pressing it
(<Ui id="transpose.reset" />) puts the song back the way it arrived. Twelve steps
is the same music an octave away, so the twelfth wraps around to `0`.

Nothing about this leaves your phone. The performer's screen does not move, the
next viewer's does not either, and no song anywhere is rewritten — to change a
song for good you need the editor, which
[Transpose a song](./transpose-a-song.mdx) is about. Your offset holds for the
rest of the evening and is gone when you leave the lobby.

## 11. Re-sync after your phone was locked

A phone that was locked, or off in another app, catches up by itself when you
come back to it. The lobby's current song is stored, not just broadcast, so a
missed message is not a missed song.

The menu's <Ui id="audience.sync" /> is for the case where you suspect you are
behind and would rather not wait to find out: it re-reads the lobby and applies
it. Pressing it when you are already current does nothing at all, so it is
always safe to press.
