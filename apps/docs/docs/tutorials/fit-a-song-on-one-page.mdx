# Fit a song on one page

One song, one page, no scrolling while you play.

This is a repair job rather than a tour. At the end of the link below there is a
song that has been set up wrong on purpose — six columns for five verses, a
white border you could park a car in, the words shoved into the bottom-right
corner and the title running up the left edge like the back of a book. Import
it and you will be looking at a page nobody could play from.

Then you take it apart one setting at a time and watch the page answer.

That is the whole of this step. The mechanics — what a link carries, what the

What matters here is what arrived with it. **A song carries its own render
settings.** They belong to the song the way its words do: they travelled in the
link, they landed in your library, and they will be on your phone after it
syncs. That is why this song looks broken on your machine and not only on mine
— and it is why, once you have fixed it, it stays fixed everywhere it turns up.

## 2. Open its settings

Open the song from <AppLink to="songs">Songs</AppLink> — the pencil on its row,
or a double-click — and the screen splits: the source on the left, the page it
draws on the right.

The gear at the far end of the toolbar is the one you want. Its tooltip
reads <Ui id="editor.settings" />, and `Alt` + `,` opens it without the mouse.

It opens **over the source, not over the render**. There is no backdrop and
nothing is dimmed: the page on the right keeps drawing while you work, so every
number you nudge is answered in the same breath. That is the method of this
whole tutorial — do not tune blind and check afterwards, watch it.

<Image
  alt="The render settings open over the demo song's source, with the broken page still drawing beside them"
  src="/img/screenshot-song-settings.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-song-settings-dark.png"
  shadow
/>

Read the panel as a list of what this song has an opinion about. A row the song
is not setting reads <Ui id="settings.inherited" /> and is taking its value from
your global one; a row it _is_ setting shows the value and a reset button
(<Ui id="settings.reset" />) where the badge was. Almost the whole
of <Ui id="settingGroup.page" /> shows a value here, and that is precisely what
is wrong with the page beside it.

One row to notice and then leave alone: <Ui id="setting.scale" /> is
on <Ui id="scale.auto" />, where it stays for the rest of this tutorial and for
almost every song you will ever set. It is not a size anybody chose — it is the
largest size at which the whole song still fits its page, recomputed every time
anything else moves. Which is why the text jumps every time one of the steps
below hands the page some room back.

:::note[On a phone]

There is only one pane at a time, and a button in the bar switches between them.
The settings still open over the source, so this is the one place where you tune
first and look second.

:::

## 3. Set the aspect ratio

The frame first: everything after this is measured inside it.

<Ui id="setting.aspectRatio" /> is the **shape** of the page, not its size. The
demo song arrives on <Ui id="aspect.a4" />, which is the shape of paper — and
you are almost certainly looking at a screen that is nothing like it. A good
part of the white you are staring at is that mismatch and nothing else.

The picker has four shelves:

- <Ui id="aspect.group.device" /> — <Ui id="aspect.matchScreen" /> measures the
  device you are holding and stores the ratio it measured, so a phone saves
  something like `131:284`, and that keeps meaning _that phone_ after it syncs
  to your desktop. Its sideways twin, <Ui id="aspect.matchScreenSideways" />, is
  the same measurement turned over — which is how you get a landscape song
  without physically rotating a device that has rotation lock on.
- <Ui id="aspect.group.paper" /> — <Ui id="aspect.a4" /> and
  <Ui id="aspect.letter" />, for a song that is going to be printed.
- <Ui id="aspect.group.portrait" /> — `9:19.5` down through `3:4`, tallest
  first, ending at <Ui id="aspect.square" />.
- <Ui id="aspect.group.landscape" /> — `21:9` down to `4:3`, which is the shelf
  laptop screens live on.

Take the shelf that matches what you will play from. The pictures on this page
were taken at `16:9`, off a laptop.

Every named ratio carries its numbers in its label, so you can check the claim
before you act on it. Below the list is a free-text field: it takes a ratio like
`3:4`, a fraction like `3/4`, a plain number, or the word `A4`, and it turns
away anything the renderer could not draw rather than storing a value that would
quietly do nothing.

:::note[Shape, not size]

Printing keeps the shape and scales it to fit the paper. Choosing a phone ratio
does not make a small PDF — it makes a tall, narrow one.

:::

## 4. Bring the columns back

<Ui id="setting.columns" /> arrived at `6`, and the song has five blocks. That
is the silliest thing on the page: one column has nothing to put in it, and the
other five are too narrow to hold a line of lyrics, so the fit scale collapses
and the whole song is drawn at the size of a footnote.

Take it to `2`.

The renderer chooses **where** to break, and it only ever breaks between blocks:
a verse is never sawn in half, and the columns are balanced so that the tallest
one comes out as short as it can. A song written as one long block therefore has
nowhere to break, and setting it to two columns changes nothing whatsoever — a
blank line is what gives the renderer a choice.

Columns are for a song that is **taller** than its page: many short lines. This
one is exactly that, which is why two of them is a win here. Where the lines are
long instead, two of them side by side need twice the width, and the fit scale
is what pays for it — the song comes out smaller rather than bigger. Press the
stepper and look; the render answers in the pane beside you.

## 5. Trim the padding

<Ui id="setting.padding" /> arrived at `4`, its maximum — a white border four
text sizes deep on every edge of a page that was mostly white already. Take it
to `0.5`.

The border is _inside_ the page, so it never bends the shape you chose in step
3; it only pushes the song away from the edges. Which means what you take off it
is room the song gets, and with the scale on <Ui id="scale.auto" /> that room
comes straight back as bigger letters.

It runs `0` to `4` in quarters. Like the block gap it is measured in text sizes
rather than millimetres, so it holds its proportion however big the song is
printed. `0` runs the song to the paper's edge — a fine thing on a screen you
read from a metre away, and a poor one on a page you are about to print.

:::note[A book adds its own]

A songbook's print margin is added on top of this, not instead of it. See
[Print a songbook](./print-a-songbook.mdx).

:::

## 6. Put the song where it belongs

The song is down in the bottom-right corner because it was told to be. Two rows
say so, and they are independent of each other:

- <Ui id="setting.contentX" /> — across the page:
  <Ui id="setting.contentX.left" />, <Ui id="setting.contentX.center" />,
  <Ui id="setting.contentX.right" />.
- <Ui id="setting.contentY" /> — down it: <Ui id="setting.contentY.top" />,
  <Ui id="setting.contentY.middle" />, <Ui id="setting.contentY.bottom" />.

Press <Ui id="setting.contentX.center" /> and <Ui id="setting.contentY.middle" />,
which is where both of them start.

Neither row does anything to a song that already fills its page: there is no
leftover room to place. So the closer the last three steps have brought this
song to fitting, the less these two have left to do — which is worth knowing
before you decide the buttons are broken. The corner-hugging pair is not
useless, though. Setting <Ui id="setting.contentX.left" />
with <Ui id="setting.contentY.top" /> is what you want when the song shares a
screen with something else.

## 7. Bring the title off the spine

<Ui id="setting.titlePosition" /> is on <Ui id="setting.titlePosition.left" />,
the rotated one: the title runs up the left edge like the spine of a book. It
costs a strip of width and gives back the whole height the title was using,
which on a short, wide page is a real trade rather than a decoration. On the
screen you play from it is a title you have to tilt your head to read.

<Ui id="setting.titlePosition.top" /> puts it back across the page, which is
where this song wants it. Either way the title block hugs the top-left corner —
the setting turns it, it does not move it.

Its neighbour, <Ui id="setting.titleLayout" />, decides whether the subtitle
sits under the title or beside it: <Ui id="setting.titleLayout.stacked" />
or <Ui id="setting.titleLayout.inline" />. Beside it is one line instead of two,
which is one more line for the song. The demo song leaves that row alone, so it
is one of the few still reading <Ui id="settings.inherited" />.

## 8. Open the block gap until it reads

Every step so far has taken room away from the page and handed it to the words.
This one spends some of it back, and it is the reason to have won it.

<Ui id="setting.blockGap" /> is the air between blocks — the space that
separates a verse from a chorus. It arrived at `4`: four whole lines of lyrics
between every pair of blocks, which on a five-block song is sixteen lines of
page spent on nothing at all. The default is `0.8`, the stepper moves in tenths,
and `0` is a real answer that makes the blocks abut.

Come down from `4` and stop where the verses look like verses instead of one run
of text. On this song that is somewhere around `1.2`.

Measured in lines of lyrics rather than in millimetres, the gap keeps its
proportion however big the song ends up being drawn — so the density you settle
on here is the density you get on a phone, in a book, and in a PDF.

## 9. Size the chords to taste

<Ui id="setting.chordSize" /> is the size of the chords **relative to the
lyrics**, from `0.5` to `3`. Mostly a matter of taste, and not a free one.

A chord rides in a row of its own above the line it belongs to, and that row is
as tall as the chords in it. Shrink them and every line of the song that carries
any gets shorter; grow them and the song gets taller, and the fit scale answers
by drawing everything smaller. If you play from across the room and want the
chords big, this is the row where the page pays for them.

<Ui id="setting.chordColor" /> sits beside it and changes nothing about the fit:
it is the colour every chord in the song is drawn in.

:::note[Notation is not a rewrite]

The same section carries <Ui id="setting.notation" />, which spells the printed
chords in English or German note names. It never touches what you wrote —
[Transpose a song](./transpose-a-song.mdx) covers that.

:::

That is the repair. Same song, same words, same eight settings — a page you
could hand to somebody and ask them to play from.

## 10. Keep it, or make it the house style

Everything above was set on **one song**, which is the right scope for a song
with a reason. The other two scopes are for when it has not got one.

- **Global** — <Ui id="nav.settings" /> ▸ <Ui id="settings.rendering" /> is the
  same panel with every row in it, and it is the base every song starts from.
  Set your usual page shape and your usual block gap there once, and new songs
  arrive already looking the way you like.
- **A songbook** — a book carries the few settings that make a house style: the
  fonts, the chord colour and size, the block gap, and the notation. That is
  [Build a songbook](./build-a-songbook.mdx#13-restyle-the-chords-for-the-whole-book).
- **A song** — everything, and the last word.

The order is always global, then songbook, then song. A song that sets a value
for itself beats the book it is in, and the book beats your global default —
which is all a row marked <Ui id="settings.inherited" /> is saying: nobody
nearer than the global has an opinion about it yet.

Clearing an override hands the setting back up that chain. At global scope there
is nothing above to hand it to, so the reset there
reads <Ui id="settings.resetDefault" /> and puts back the value Achordeon ships
with.

The demo song has served its purpose by now. Keep it as a scratch page to
experiment on, or delete it from <AppLink to="songs">Songs</AppLink> — the link
above will always hand you a fresh, freshly broken copy.
