# Fit a song on one page

One song, one page, no scrolling while you play.

The words are half of what you see. The other half is the page they are drawn
on — its shape, the size of the text, the border around it, the air between the
verses. Those are the **render settings**, and they belong to the song, so a
song you have made fit stays fitted everywhere it turns up: in the editor, in a
songbook, on stage, on a phone in the audience.

They also compose, which is the thing worth learning here. Left on <Ui id="scale.auto" />,
the scale fills the page as far as it will go — so room you take out of the gap
between blocks, or off the border, does not stay empty. It comes back as bigger
text.

## 1. Open the song's settings

Everything below happens in the editor. Open the song from <AppLink to="songs">Songs</AppLink> —
the pencil on its row, or a double-click — and the screen splits: the source on
the left, the page it draws on the right.

The gear at the far end of the toolbar is the one you want. Its tooltip reads <Ui id="editor.settings" />,
and `Alt` + `,` opens it without the mouse.

It opens **over the source, not over the render**. There is no backdrop and
nothing is dimmed: the page on the right keeps drawing while you work, so every
number you nudge is answered in the same breath. That is the method of this
whole tutorial — do not tune blind and check afterwards, watch it.

The panel is four sections — <Ui id="settingGroup.page" />, <Ui id="settingGroup.title" />, <Ui id="settingGroup.fonts" />
and <Ui id="settingGroup.chords" /> — and the first of them holds almost
everything this tutorial touches. Each row carries a `?` that says what the
setting does.

Every row starts out marked <Ui id="settings.inherited" />: the song is not
setting it, and the value is coming from your global one. The moment you touch
it the badge becomes a reset button (<Ui id="settings.reset" />), which is also
how you hand the setting back later.

:::note[On a phone]
There is only one pane at a time, and a button in the bar switches between them.
The settings still open over the source, so this is the one place where you tune
first and look second.
:::

## 2. Set the aspect ratio to the screen you play from

<Ui id="setting.aspectRatio" /> is the **shape** of the page, not its size. It
starts on <Ui id="aspect.a4" />, which is a compromise: that is the shape of
paper, and you are almost certainly looking at a screen that is nothing like it.

The picker has four shelves:

- <Ui id="aspect.group.device" /> — <Ui id="aspect.matchScreen" /> measures the
  device you are holding and stores the ratio it measured, so a phone saves
  something like `131:284`, and that keeps meaning _that phone_ after it syncs
  to your desktop. Its sideways twin, <Ui id="aspect.matchScreenSideways" />, is
  the same measurement turned over — which is how you get a landscape song
  without physically rotating a device that has rotation lock on.
- <Ui id="aspect.group.paper" /> — <Ui id="aspect.a4" /> and
  <Ui id="aspect.letter" />, for a song that is going to be printed.
- <Ui id="aspect.group.portrait" /> — `9:19.5` down through `3:4`, tallest
  first, ending at <Ui id="aspect.square" />.
- <Ui id="aspect.group.landscape" /> — `21:9` down to `4:3`, which is the shelf
  laptop screens live on.

Every named ratio carries its numbers in its label, so you can check the claim
before you act on it. Below the list is a free-text field: it takes a ratio like
`3:4`, a fraction like `3/4`, a plain number, or the word `A4`, and it turns
away anything the renderer could not draw rather than storing a value that would
quietly do nothing.

:::note[Shape, not size]
Printing keeps the shape and scales it to fit the paper. Choosing a phone ratio
does not make a small PDF — it makes a tall, narrow one.
:::

The fastest win on this page is often right here: crop the page to the song,
instead of leaving A4's margins for the renderer to fill with nothing.

## 3. Close up the block gap

<Ui id="setting.blockGap" /> is the space that separates a verse from a chorus.
It is measured in lines of lyrics rather than in millimetres, so it keeps its
proportion however big the song ends up being drawn.

The default is `0.8` of a line and the stepper moves in tenths. `0` is a real
answer — the blocks abut — and on a song of five blocks, a gap you close up is
four lines of page you get back.

It is the cheapest lever on the whole panel, and the reason is the composition
rule from the top: with the scale left on <Ui id="scale.auto" />, the room you
take out here does not sit empty. It is spent on making every letter bigger.

## 4. Split the song into columns

<Ui id="setting.columns" /> cuts the song into side-by-side columns, up to six
of them.

The renderer chooses **where** to break, and it only ever breaks between blocks:
a verse is never sawn in half, and the columns are balanced so that the tallest
one comes out as short as it can. A song written as one long block therefore has
nowhere to break, and setting it to two columns changes nothing whatsoever — a
blank line is what gives the renderer a choice.

Columns are for a song that is **taller** than its page: many short lines. Where
the lines are long, two of them side by side need twice the width, and the fit
scale is what pays for it — the song comes out smaller rather than bigger. Press
the stepper and look; the render answers in the pane beside you.

## 5. Trim the padding

<Ui id="setting.padding" /> is the white border between the song and the edge of
the page, from `0` to `4` in quarters. Like the block gap it is measured in text
sizes, so it holds its proportion however big the song is printed.

It is the same trade as step 3 and it behaves the same way: what you take off
the border comes back as text size. Take it to `0` and the song runs to the
paper's edge — a fine thing on a screen you are reading from a metre away, and a
poor one on a page you are about to print.

:::note[A book adds its own]
A songbook's print margin is added on top of this, not instead of it. See
[Print a songbook](./print-a-songbook.mdx).
:::

## 6. Set the scale, or leave it on auto

<Ui id="setting.scale" /> is how big the text is, and it is the setting the
three before it have been feeding all along.

Left on <Ui id="scale.auto" />, it is not a number you chose. It is the largest
size at which the whole song still fits its page, recomputed every time anything
else moves. That is why closing the block gap makes the letters grow, and why
two columns of long lines makes them shrink: you are not editing the size, you
are editing how much room the size has to work with.

Type a number instead and you have taken the wheel. `1` is the base size, the
stepper moves in hundredths, and the song is drawn at exactly that whether or
not it fits — a fixed scale is allowed to overflow the page. The reason to want
one is consistency across a set: two songs at the same fixed scale have the same
lettering, where two songs on <Ui id="scale.auto" /> almost certainly do not.

The <Ui id="scale.auto" /> button beside the field hands it back.

## 7. Place the song on the page

Once a song fits it usually does not fill, and something has to decide where the
leftover room goes. Two rows answer that, and they are independent of each
other:

- <Ui id="setting.contentX" /> — across the page:
  <Ui id="setting.contentX.left" />,
  <Ui id="setting.contentX.center" />, <Ui id="setting.contentX.right" />.
- <Ui id="setting.contentY" /> — down it: <Ui id="setting.contentY.top" />,
  <Ui id="setting.contentY.middle" />, <Ui id="setting.contentY.bottom" />.

Both start in the middle.

Neither does anything to a song that already fills its page, which is worth
knowing before you decide the buttons are broken. The corner-hugging pair, <Ui id="setting.contentX.left" />
and <Ui id="setting.contentY.top" />, is what you want when the song shares the
screen with something else.

## 8. Move the title

The title block — the title and its subtitle together — has two settings of its
own.

<Ui id="setting.titlePosition" /> puts it <Ui id="setting.titlePosition.top" />,
across the top of the page, or turns it into a
<Ui id="setting.titlePosition.left" />: rotated, running up the left edge like
the spine of a book. The spine costs a strip of width and gives back the whole
height the title was using, which on a short, wide page is a real trade rather
than a decoration. Either way the block hugs the top-left corner.

<Ui id="setting.titleLayout" /> decides whether the subtitle sits under the
title or beside it — <Ui id="setting.titleLayout.stacked" /> or
<Ui id="setting.titleLayout.inline" />. Beside it is one line instead of two,
which is one more line for the song.

## 9. Size the chords

<Ui id="setting.chordSize" /> is the size of the chords **relative to the
lyrics**, from `0.5` to `3`.

It is not only a matter of taste. A chord rides in a row of its own above the
line it belongs to, and that row is as tall as the chords in it — so shrinking
the chords shortens every line of the song that carries any, and growing them
lengthens it. If you play from across the room and want the chords big, this is
where the page pays for them.

<Ui id="setting.chordColor" /> is in the same section and changes nothing about
the fit: it is the colour every chord in the song is drawn in.

:::note[Notation is not a rewrite]
The same section carries <Ui id="setting.notation" />, which spells the printed
chords in English or German note names. It never touches what you wrote —
[Transpose a song](./transpose-a-song.mdx) covers that.
:::

## 10. Keep the setting for one song, or make it global

Everything above was set on **one song**, which is the right scope for a song
with a reason. The other two scopes are for when it is not.

- **Global** — <Ui id="nav.settings" /> ▸ <Ui id="settings.rendering" /> is the
  same panel with every row in it, and it is the base every song starts from.
  Set your usual page shape and your usual block gap there once, and new songs
  arrive already looking the way you like.
- **A songbook** — a book carries the few settings that make a house style: the
  fonts, the chord colour and size, the block gap, and the notation. That is
  [Build a songbook](./build-a-songbook.mdx#13-restyle-the-chords-for-the-whole-book).
- **A song** — everything, and the last word.

The order is always global, then songbook, then song. A song that sets a value
for itself beats the book it is in, and the book beats your global default —
which is all a row marked <Ui id="settings.inherited" /> is saying: nobody
nearer than the global has an opinion about it yet.

Clearing an override hands the setting back up that chain. At global scope there
is nothing above to hand it to, so the reset there reads <Ui id="settings.resetDefault" />
and puts back the value Achordeon ships with.
