# Build a songbook

An ordered list of songs, arranged the way the evening runs.

A songbook does not copy your songs. It holds positions that point at them, so a
song edited anywhere is edited everywhere it appears, and the same song can sit
in a book twice without costing anything twice.

## 1. Open `Songbooks`

The module is in the rail on the left — <AppLink to="songbooks">open it
now</AppLink>. It lists your books, with <Ui id="songbooks.allSongs" /> at the
top: the whole
library as one read-only book. That one you cannot rename, reorder or delete;
everything below it is yours.

Click a book to see its title page in the pane beside the list, double-click to
open it. On a phone there is no second pane, so one tap opens it.

<Image
  alt="The Songbooks module, listing the books over the title page of the selected one"
  src="/img/screenshot-songbooks.png"
/>

## 2. Create a songbook

Press <Ui id="songbooks.add" icon="+" /> in the header. An empty book appears in
the list, named <Ui id="songbooks.newName" />, and the app takes you straight
into it.

## 3. Name it

It arrived called <Ui id="songbooks.newName" />, which is no use once there are
two of them.
The book's heading is the name field: click it, type, and press Enter. Escape
puts back what was there. You can rename it from the list too, without opening
it — the pencil on the row does the same thing.

The name is what you will search for later, so it is worth being the name of the
evening rather than of the month: _Campfire_, _Wedding — Friday_, _Set 2_.

## 4. Select songs in the left list

An open book is two lists side by side: your library on the left, the book
itself on the right. The left list is the <Ui id="nav.songs" /> module's own
explorer, with the acts that do not belong here switched off.

Tick the rows you want. The tick is a separate gesture from opening a row, so a
selection survives everything else you do — you can scroll, search, and tick
some more before you commit to anything.

## 5. Insert them into the book

Between the two lists is a small column with a `+` and a `−`. The `+` puts
everything you ticked into the book **below the slot you have picked there**, or
at the end when you have picked none.

There is one Add button, not four. Hover it and the book draws a line where the
songs would land, so you can see the answer before you press; if it is not the
answer you wanted, the reorder buttons in the next step nudge it from there.

<Image
  alt="A songbook open in the builder: the library on the left, the book on the right, the transfer column between them"
  src="/img/screenshot-songbook-detail.png"
/>

## 6. Drag songs in instead

Every row has a drag handle before its tick, and the handle is the only part of
the row that drags — so a press that was meant to select never turns into a
move. Drag a selected row and the whole selection comes with it. The book draws
the same landing line as the `+` does, and the drop lands there.

## 7. Reorder the book

Tick slots in the book and use the four move buttons: to the start, up one, down
one, to the end. They act on the whole selection, so a run of songs moves as a
run. Dragging by the handle works here too.

The buttons are dead while nothing is ticked, and say so if you hover them.

<Image
  alt="The four reorder buttons: move to the start, up one, down one, to the end"
  src="/img/screenshot-songbook-reorder.png"
/>

## 8. Add the same song twice

Nothing stops you, and nothing is duplicated when you do. A book of a reprise,
an encore, or a song you open and close the night with is a book with that song
in two positions.

The two positions are independent — removing one leaves the other alone.

## 9. Preview a song without leaving the list

Any row, in either list, opens the song's render in a dialog. It is the real
render, the same one the page would be printed from, so it answers the question
you actually have at this point: does this song fit where I am putting it.

<Image
  alt="A song's render open in a dialog over the songbook builder"
  src="/img/screenshot-songbook-preview.png"
/>

## 10. Edit a song from the preview and come back

The dialog has an <Ui id="songbooks.previewEdit" /> button. It takes you into
the song's own editor, and <Ui id="songbooks.previewClose" /> there brings you
back to the songbook exactly where you left it — same selection, same scroll,
same song still previewed.

That is deliberate: fixing a chord you noticed mid-build should cost you the fix
and nothing else. The trail home is in the URL, so a reload does not lose it
either.

## 11. Jump to a song with the songbook search

The book has its own search above it, separate from the library's. It does not
filter: filtering a book you are arranging would hide the slot you were looking
at. Instead it **jumps** to the next entry that matches — by name, title,
subtitle or lyrics.

A counter shows which match you are on. Enter and the arrows step through them,
Escape clears. Like every search in the app, it ignores accents, so `nekdo`
finds _Někdo_.

## 12. Remove a song from the book

Tick the slots and press `−`, the button under the `+`.

:::note[Remove vs delete]
Removing a song from a songbook won't delete the song. The song can be deleted
only in the <Ui id="nav.songs" /> module.
:::

## 13. Restyle the chords for the whole book

<Ui id="songbooks.settings" /> in the book's header — also on its row in the
list — opens the songbook's own scope of the render settings: the fonts (the
song's, the title's, and the italic it borrows), chord colour and chord size,
the block gap, and the notation. Set one here and every song in the book follows
it, without any of those songs being changed.

Every field reads <Ui id="settings.inherited" /> until you touch it — the setting is still coming
from the global one, and clearing it hands it back.

A song that sets the same thing for itself still wins. The order is always
global, then songbook, then song: the book imposes a house style, and a song is
allowed to have a reason.

The same dialog carries the book's title page and how it is put together when
printed — see [Songbooks](/songbooks/index.mdx) for what each field does.
