# Print a songbook

The book as paper: cover, contents, numbers, PDF.

Two different things decide what comes off the printer, and telling them apart
is most of this page.

The book's **structure** — whether there is a title page, what it looks like,
whether there is a contents list, where the page numbers sit — belongs to the
songbook. "This hymnal has a contents page" is a fact about the hymnal, so it is
stored on the book, it syncs, and it is already set the next time you print from
another device.

The **paper** — A4 or Letter, portrait or landscape, how wide a margin — belongs
to the machine you are printing from, and is remembered there.

So the structure is set once, in the book's settings, and the download dialog
asks only what the book cannot know: what you are about to print it on.

## 1. Open the songbook's settings

<Ui id="songbooks.settings" /> is the gear in an open book's header, and it is
also <Ui id="explorer.configure" /> on the book's row in the list — the same
dialog either way. It is already pictured in [Build a
songbook](./build-a-songbook.mdx#13-restyle-the-chords-for-the-whole-book),
which is where the bottom half of it — the fonts, the chord colour, the notation
— is explained.

This page is about the two sections above that panel: <Ui id="songbooks.titlePage" />,
which is what the front sheet says, and <Ui id="songbooks.print" />, which is
what the book is made of.

Open it from the **list** rather than from inside the book if you can. There is
no backdrop and nothing is dimmed, so the pane beside it keeps drawing the book
as pages while you work — which is step 8, happening continuously instead of at
the end.

:::note[All songs is different]

<Ui id="songbooks.allSongs" /> has no record of its own, so it has no title,
subtitle or author to print, and its dialog is the print section alone plus the
order it is arranged in. Everything else on this page applies to it unchanged,
and what it sets is kept on the device rather than synced.

:::

## 2. Switch on the title page

<Ui id="songbookDownload.titlePage" /> is the first row of
<Ui id="songbooks.print" />, and a book that has said nothing already has it on.
Off, the file starts at the first song.

Every toggle in this section hides the row under it while it is off, which is
worth knowing before you go hunting for a setting that is not missing. The style
picker exists only while there is a title page, the numbering row only while
there is a contents list.

## 3. Fill in the title, subtitle and author

The three fields are in the <Ui id="songbooks.titlePage" /> section above, and
the `?` beside its heading says the one thing worth saying about them:
_Printed on the songbook's title page. Separate from any song's own title._

- <Ui id="songbooks.field.title" /> — what the sheet says in large letters.
  Leave it empty and the book's **name** is printed instead, so a book always
  has something on its cover; fill it in when the name you filed it under is not
  the name you want on paper.
- <Ui id="songbooks.field.subtitle" /> — the line under it.
- <Ui id="songbooks.field.author" /> — you, the band, the parish.

A field you leave blank is not printed as an empty line — it is dropped, and the
sheet is composed out of what is actually there. That is why an author you have
not filled in costs you nothing, and is still worth filling in: several of the
styles in the next step are built around having one.

The title page is a **render**, like every other page in the book, so it is set
in the book's own fonts. It also carries a small `achordeon.eu`, placed as part
of each style's own design. That mark is the app's rather than your book's, so no
style leaves it out and no setting turns it off.

## 4. Pick a title page style

<Ui id="songbookDownload.variant" /> offers twenty-one, and every one of them is
drawn — there is nothing in the list that cannot be chosen. They are ordered by
how far each departs from the shipped page: the quiet ones first, the ones that
put a shape on the paper last, so reading down the list is walking a scale.

They divide in two. Ten are **placement alone** — the same words, arranged
differently:

- <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.classic" /> is what an untouched book gets:
  the three fields as one left-aligned block, centred on the sheet.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.plate" /> puts a small title high on the page
  and the author at the foot, with the space between them left empty. The
  hymnal.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.poster" /> sets the title as large as the
  width allows. One thing to read from across a room.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.minimal" /> prints the title alone, at body
  size, in a corner — and nothing else, including an author the book has.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.spine" /> reads the title up the left edge,
  the same quarter turn everything sideways in Achordeon takes.

The other eleven put **ink** on the paper as well. <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.rule" />
draws a line under the title and <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.marquee" /> one
above it and below it; <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.framed" /> is a border
inset from the page edge; <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.banner" /> is a filled
band across the upper third with the title reversed out of it; and <Ui id="songbookDownload.variant.ticket" />
is a small rounded box holding the whole book — the one style that also prints how
many songs it binds.

The rest are named for what they look like, and the fastest way to choose is not
to read about them: pick one and watch the pane beside you. The full list, with a
line on each, is in [Songbooks](/songbooks/index.mdx).

## 5. Switch on the summary

<Ui id="songbookDownload.summary" /> adds a contents page, and unlike the title
page it is **off** until you ask for it.

It is a list of every song against the page it is printed on, laid out in two
columns — one column of a forty-song book wastes half of an A4 and runs onto a
second sheet it does not need.

The numbers are page numbers, and where they start is the point: **the title page
and the contents page carry no number themselves, so the first song is page 1.**
Numbering the front matter would make the contents send a reader to page 3 for
the first song, which is a number they can only use by counting past two sheets
that also claim one.

In a PDF the whole line is a link — the title, the run of leader dots and the
number alike — so tapping anywhere along it jumps to the song. It is the one page
of the book that is not a render: it is drawn as PDF text, because a page number
is only knowable once the pagination has been decided.

## 6. Choose which side the page number sits on

That is the number in the **contents**, not the one on the song's own page. The
row is <Ui id="songbookDownload.summaryNumber" />, and there are two answers:

- <Ui id="songbookDownload.afterTitle" /> is the reference table: `Wonderwall ·
  · · · · 7`, the number against the right edge of its column under a run of
  dots, which is what an eye runs along when it has a name and wants a page.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.beforeTitle" /> is the hymnal: `7. Wonderwall`, the
  numbers in a column of their own with every title indented past the widest of
  them, which is what you read when somebody has called out a number.

One or the other, never both, and that is a decision rather than a missing
checkbox: the book numbers each song by its page, so a contents line carrying the
number at each end says the same thing twice on every row.

## 7. Switch on page numbers and place them

<Ui id="songbookDownload.pageNumbers" /> is on by default, and the row under it,
<Ui id="songbookDownload.position" />, offers seven places. Six of them are a
spot on the paper — bottom or top, left, right or centred.

The seventh is not a spot at all. <Ui id="songbookDownload.beforeSongTitle" />
puts the number into the song's own heading — `7. Wonderwall` — which is how a
book is used out loud, and what survives a page being photocopied or re-bound.
Because it is part of the title it is drawn by the renderer rather than stamped
on afterwards, so it wears the title's face and follows the title onto the left
edge when the song's own settings put it there. Nothing is then printed in the
corner as well: it is one number in one place, not a number and its echo.

Whichever you choose, the numbers count from the first **song**, exactly as the
contents does, and they are never padded — 1, 2, … 10, 11, because `07` on a
printed page reads as a serial number rather than as the seventh song.

:::note[If the book is going to be bound]

Achordeon prints the number in the same corner on every sheet — there is no odd
page and even page. Bind the sheets back to back and a fixed <Ui id="songbookDownload.bottomRight" />
lands on the outer edge of one page of the spread and against the spine on the
other. <Ui id="songbookDownload.bottomCenter" /> is the default for that reason:
it is the one position a binding cannot swallow.

:::

## 8. Check the preview

Go back to <AppLink to="songbooks">Songbooks</AppLink> and click the book. The
pane beside the list is not its cover — it is **the whole book as pages**, every
sheet the PDF would hold, in order: the title page, the contents, then the songs
with their numbers in the corner you chose.

It is a real scroll box, so the scrollbar, the trackpad and PageDown all work the
way they do everywhere else, and the readout in the bar under the paper says
which sheets you are looking at and how many there are. Nothing snaps: a view
resting halfway between two sheets is your business.

The pair of buttons on the right is <Ui id="songbookPreview.zoom" />, and what it
moves is a **column count**. It reads as a magnifier, so the two work the other
way round from their sign: <Ui id="songbookPreview.zoomIn" /> shows fewer, larger
pages, and <Ui id="songbookPreview.zoomOut" /> shows more, smaller ones, up to
the whole book at once as a contact sheet. A sheet is not drawn until you are
nearly at it, which is what keeps a two-hundred-page book cheap to scroll
through; a frame with a turning ring in it is paper that has not been inked yet,
not a page that failed.

The moon on the left is <Ui id="stage.darkPage" />, and the caption that appears
beside it says what it is: <Ui id="songbookPreview.notPrint" />. It turns the
paper over for reading in the dark, it is separate from the app's own theme, and
it does not reach the PDF.

## 10. Choose the format

<Ui id="songbookDownload.format" /> decides which of the rows below it are even
asked, because only one of the four is about paper.

| Format                                     | What you get                                                                                                                                | Opens again in Achordeon                  |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| <Ui id="songbookDownload.format.pdf" />    | The whole book as one document: title page, contents, songs, numbers.                                                                       | no                                        |
| <Ui id="songbookDownload.format.zipPng" /> | One image per song, numbered in book order, plus a contents page. No title sheet and no corner numbers — a folder of pictures has no paper. | yes — each PNG carries its song inside it |
| <Ui id="songbookDownload.format.data" />   | The songbook **and its songs**, as data.                                                                                                    | yes                                       |
| <Ui id="songbookDownload.format.link" />   | The same, packed into a URL and copied ready to paste. Nothing is uploaded.                                                                 | yes                                       |

The link is the one that can be refused. It is greyed rather than removed when
the book will not fit in one, with the reason beside it: <Ui id="songbookDownload.linkTooBig" />
It is a length rather than a song count, so one long song can trip it where three
short ones would not.

Two of the four ignore everything below. The Achordeon file and the link are the
book as data, and neither has an opinion about paper, so the dialog becomes one
row and a button — which for the link reads <Ui id="songbookDownload.copyLink" />
rather than <Ui id="songbookDownload.confirm" />.

## 11. Set the paper size, orientation and margin

For a PDF, three more rows:

- <Ui id="songbookDownload.pageSize" /> — `A4`, `Letter` or `A5`.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.orientation" /> — portrait or landscape, which is the
  same paper turned over.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.margin" /> — the blank edge of the sheet, in whole
  millimetres, starting at `0`.

Nothing here reshapes a song. Each one keeps the aspect ratio it was given in its
own settings and is **scaled to fit** what the margin leaves, centred, so a song
shaped unlike the paper sits with white either side rather than being stretched
to fill it. A song wider than it is tall is laid on its side automatically when
turning gains it room — there is no setting for that, because a sheet of paper
always turns.

:::note[The margin and the song's padding are both there]

A song's own <Ui id="setting.padding" /> is a border measured in text sizes,
inside the render; the book's margin is millimetres taken off the sheet before
the render is placed on it. They **add** — but they do not fight, because the
song is scaled into whatever is left: a wider margin does not push the padding
off the page, it makes the whole song, its padding included, smaller. So set the
margin for the printer's unprintable edge and for whatever binding you are
planning, and set the padding for how the song itself looks. See
[Fit a song on one page](./fit-a-song-on-one-page.mdx#5-trim-the-padding).

:::

Your paper is remembered on this device, so the next book opens the dialog on the
same settings. Taking the Achordeon file or the link is not remembered, because
neither says anything about paper.

## 12. Save the file

Press <Ui id="songbookDownload.confirm" />. The button becomes the progress — a
spinner, and once the count is known an "n of N" that advances as the book
renders — and it cannot be pressed twice.

Where the file lands is the browser's business rather than the app's. Chromium
opens the system save dialog, so it goes where you put it; Firefox and Safari
send it to your downloads folder. Either way it is named after the book:
`sunday-set.pdf`, or `sunday-set.zip` for the images.

That file is finished. Nothing in it is a link back to Achordeon and nothing in
it needs the app to read it — which is the whole reason to make one.
