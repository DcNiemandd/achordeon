# Print a songbook

The book as paper: cover, contents, numbers, PDF.

Two different things decide what comes off the printer, and telling them apart
is most of this page.

The book's **structure** — whether there is a title page, what it looks like,
whether there is a contents list, where the page numbers sit — belongs to the
songbook. "This hymnal has a contents page" is a fact about the hymnal, so it is
stored on the book, it syncs, and it is already set the next time you print from
another device.

The **paper** — A4 or Letter, portrait or landscape, how wide a margin — belongs
to the machine you are printing from, and is remembered there.

So the structure is set once, in the book's settings, and the download dialog
asks only what the book cannot know: what you are about to print it on.

## 1. Open the songbook's settings

<Ui id="songbooks.settings" /> is the gear in an open book's header, and it is
also <Ui id="explorer.configure" /> on the book's row in the list — the same
dialog either way. It is already pictured in [Build a
songbook](./build-a-songbook.mdx#13-restyle-the-chords-for-the-whole-book),
which is where the bottom half of it — the fonts, the chord colour, the notation
— is explained.

This page is about the two sections above that panel: <Ui id="songbooks.titlePage" />,
which is what the front sheet says, and <Ui id="songbooks.print" />, which is
what the book is made of.

Open it from the **list** rather than from inside the book if you can. There is
no backdrop and nothing is dimmed, so the pane beside it keeps drawing the book
as pages while you work — which is step 7, happening continuously instead of at
the end.

:::note[All songs is different]

<Ui id="songbooks.allSongs" /> has no record of its own, so it has no title,
subtitle or author to print, and its dialog is the print section alone plus the
order it is arranged in. Everything else on this page applies to it unchanged,
and what it sets is kept on the device rather than synced.

:::

## 2. Switch on the title page

<Ui id="songbookDownload.titlePage" /> is the first row of
<Ui id="songbooks.print" />, and a book that has said nothing already has it on.
Off, the file starts at the first song.

Every toggle in this section hides the row under it while it is off, which is
worth knowing before you go hunting for a setting that is not missing. The style
picker exists only while there is a title page, the numbering row only while
there is a contents list.

## 3. Fill in the title, subtitle and author

The three fields are in the <Ui id="songbooks.titlePage" /> section above, and
the `?` beside its heading says the one thing worth saying about them:
_Printed on the songbook's title page. Separate from any song's own title._

- <Ui id="songbooks.field.title" /> — what the sheet says in large letters.
  Leave it empty and the book's **name** is printed instead, so a book always
  has something on its cover; fill it in when the name you filed it under is not
  the name you want on paper.
- <Ui id="songbooks.field.subtitle" /> — the line under it.
- <Ui id="songbooks.field.author" /> — you, the band, the parish.

A field you leave blank is not printed as an empty line — it is dropped, and the
sheet is composed out of what is actually there. That is why an author you have
not filled in costs you nothing, and is still worth filling in: several of the
styles in the next step are built around having one.

## 4. Pick a title page style

<Ui id="songbookDownload.variant" /> offers twenty-one, and every one of them is
drawn — there is nothing in the list that cannot be chosen.

The fastest way to choose is not to read about them: pick one and watch the pane
beside you. The full list, with a line on each, is in
[Songbooks](/songbooks/index.mdx).

## 5. Switch on the summary

<Ui id="songbookDownload.summary" /> adds a contents page, and unlike the title
page it is **off** until you ask for it.

It is a list of every song against the page it is printed on, laid out in two
columns.

The numbers are page numbers, and where they start is the point: **the title page
and the contents page carry no number themselves, so the first song is page 1.**

In a PDF the whole line is a link — the title, the run of leader dots and the
number alike — so tapping anywhere along it jumps to the song.

That is the number in the **contents**, not the one on the song's own page.
The row is <Ui id="songbookDownload.summaryNumber" />, and there are two
answers: <Ui id="songbookDownload.afterTitle" /> is the reference table,
`Wonderwall · · · · · 7`, and <Ui id="songbookDownload.beforeTitle" /> is the
hymnal, `7. Wonderwall`.

## 6. Switch on page numbers and place them

<Ui id="songbookDownload.pageNumbers" /> is on by default, and the row under it,
<Ui id="songbookDownload.position" />, offers seven places. Six of them are a
spot on the paper — bottom or top, left, right or centred.

The seventh is not a spot at all. <Ui id="songbookDownload.beforeSongTitle" />
puts the number into the song's own heading — `7. Wonderwall`.

## 7. Check the preview

Go back to <AppLink to="songbooks">Songbooks</AppLink> and click the book. The
pane beside the list is not its cover — it is **the whole book as pages**, every
sheet the PDF would hold, in order: the title page, the contents, then the songs
with their numbers in the corner you chose.

## 8. Press Download

<Ui id="songbooks.download" /> is on the open book's header, beside the gear,
and the row's own <Ui id="explorer.download" /> does the same thing. There is no
separate Export button anywhere: exporting is a format inside this dialog, which
is the first thing it asks about.

## 9. Choose the format

<Ui id="songbookDownload.format" /> decides which of the rows below it are even
asked, because only one of the four is about paper. The four, with a line on
each, are in [Songbooks](/songbooks/index.mdx#download).

Two of the four ignore everything below. The Achordeon file and the link are the
book as data, and neither has an opinion about paper, so the dialog becomes one
row and a button — which for the link reads <Ui id="songbookDownload.copyLink" />
rather than <Ui id="songbookDownload.confirm" />.

## 10. Set the paper size, orientation and margin

For a PDF, three more rows:

- <Ui id="songbookDownload.pageSize" /> — `A4`, `Letter` or `A5`.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.orientation" /> — portrait or landscape, which is the
  same paper turned over.
- <Ui id="songbookDownload.margin" /> — the blank edge of the sheet, in whole
  millimetres, starting at `0`.

Your paper is remembered on this device, so the next book opens the dialog on the
same settings. Taking the Achordeon file or the link is not remembered, because
neither says anything about paper.

## 11. Save the file

Press <Ui id="songbookDownload.confirm" />. The button becomes the progress — a
spinner, and once the count is known an "n of N" that advances as the book
renders — and it cannot be pressed twice.

Where the file lands is the browser's business rather than the app's. Either way
it is named after the book: `sunday-set.pdf`, or `sunday-set.zip` for the images.

That file is finished. Nothing in it is a link back to Achordeon and nothing in
it needs the app to read it — which is the whole reason to make one.
