# Perform from a songbook

Stage mode: one song on the screen and nothing else.

A performance is not a page you visit. It is something that is **running** — a
book opened at one song, a bar of controls over it, and a record on the device
saying where you got to. It outlives a jump to another module, it outlives the
tab reloading itself, and it is over when you say it is.

:::note[The same controls, in two places]

On a wide screen the performing page draws its own bar across the top of the
song, every control in the open; the pictures here are that one. On a narrow one
there is no second bar at all — the four thumb targets at the foot of the screen
are <Ui id="stage.prev" />, <Ui id="stage.summary" />, <Ui id="stage.menu" /> and <Ui id="stage.next" />,
and everything rarer is a row inside <Ui id="stage.menu" />. Where a step below
says _the bar_, read whichever one you are holding.

:::

## 1. Start from `Stage`, or from the songbook's Perform button

The module is third in the rail — <AppLink to="stage">open it now</AppLink>. It
lists what can be performed and nothing else: no search, no sorting, no bulk
actions, because the stage is where you play from rather than where you tidy up.

You do not have to come this way. A songbook's row in <AppLink to="songbooks">Songbooks</AppLink> carries
its own <Ui id="explorer.perform" />, and an open book has one in its header
(<Ui id="songbooks.perform" />). Both land in exactly the same place, one press
earlier — a book you have just finished arranging is a book you can play without
going and finding it again.

An empty songbook is not offered anywhere. The picker leaves those out
altogether and says at the foot of the list how many it hid; a book's own
Perform is dead until there is something in it. Performing nothing is not a
useful thing to be able to do.

Each row is the book's name and how many songs are in it, with a <Ui id="stage.perform" /> at
the end. Press that, or double-click the row — the performance starts on the
first song.

<Ui id="songbooks.allSongs" /> is always the first row and always there: the
whole library as one book, for the evening that has no set list. It is the one
row with a gear beside its button, because it is the one book with no order of
its own. <Ui id="stage.allSongsOrder" /> is where you answer that, and the
options are in [Songbooks](/songbooks/index.mdx#settings).

## 3. Move to the next and previous song

<Image
  alt="A song performing: the page filling the screen, with the performing bar over it"
  src="/img/screenshot-stage-perform.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-stage-perform-dark.png"
  shadow
/>

That is the whole of stage mode. The book's name on the left, the controls
beside it, <Ui id="stage.prev" /> and <Ui id="stage.next" /> in the middle of the
bar where both thumbs can reach them, and the way out in red at the far end.

The two arrows walk the set in the order the book is in. They stop at the ends
rather than wrapping: at the first song the back arrow is dead, at the last one
the forward arrow is, so a set cannot quietly start over while you are looking at
your hands.

A page turn drops one thing and carries another. The zoom is dropped — every song
starts as the whole page (step 8) — while the key you are playing in comes with
you: a transpose set on the bar holds for the rest of the set, which is
[Transpose a song](./transpose-a-song.mdx#7-play-on--the-offset-holds-across-the-songs).

## 4. Swipe, or use the arrow keys

Both arrows have a bigger, blunter twin: the page itself.

**Swipe** across the song — right to left for the next one, left to right for the
one before. It works with a mouse held down as well as with a finger, and the
whole page is the target: there is no strip you have to find in the dark.

**The arrow keys** do the same from a keyboard, and there are four of them:
`←` and `↑` go back, `→` and `↓` go forward. Up and down as well as left and
right, so whichever pair your hands reach for — or whichever pair the pedal
under your foot was wired to send — turns the page.

The rest of what a keyboard does here is in the app's own list — press `?`, or
`g` then `h`, and look for the group called <Ui id="stage.performing" />.

## 5. Open the summary and jump to a song

Fifth song of the night and somebody shouts for the second one. <Ui id="stage.summary" /> opens
the set beside the song, numbered, with the one you are on marked in it.

<Image
  alt="The song list open over a performance: the set numbered, the current song marked, and a search over it"
  src="/img/screenshot-stage-summary.png"
  srcDark="/img/screenshot-stage-summary-dark.png"
  shadow
/>

It is a panel, not a dialog. Nothing behind it is dimmed and nothing is blocked;
the song stays where it was, and it is still the song you are performing while
you read the list.

The field at the top filters as you type, on the song's **name and its printed
title** — the two things you would be shouted at with. Tap a row and you are on
that song, with the panel closing behind you, because you asked for a song and
not for a list.

Closing it changes nothing: the button again, `Esc`, or a tap anywhere off the
panel. While it is open it takes the keyboard — the arrow keys belong to the list
and not to the set — so `Esc` first, then play on.

## 6. Go fullscreen

<Ui id="stage.enterFullscreen" /> gives the song the whole screen and asks the
device to stay awake while it has it. That second half is the one that matters
at a gig: a performer reading a verse does not touch anything for minutes, and a
phone that dims mid-song has to be woken with the hand holding the instrument.

## 7. Turn the page dark

<Ui id="stage.darkPage" /> — the moon on the bar, or the row of that name inside
<Ui id="stage.menu" /> — draws the song light-on-dark. A dark room, a stage
light, a bar at midnight: white A4 at full brightness is a lamp pointed at your
own face.

**It is the paper, not the app.** The moon is the one control that turns the
paper over, and it turns over nothing else. Your library and your editor keep
drawing on white while a dark performance is running.

You can have it decide for itself. The <Ui id="settings.app" /> section of <AppLink to="settings">Settings</AppLink> holds one checkbox, <Ui id="settings.songDark.on" />:
with it on, every song this device draws follows the theme onto a dark page.
The moon still wins over it: it is a performer saying _not on this stage_ about
the room they are actually standing in, and it holds for this performance only.
End the performance and the setting is back in charge.

:::tip[Sideways songs]

A song wider than it is tall wastes most of a phone held upright. When a quarter
turn would win one some room, a <Ui id="stage.turnPage" /> control appears beside
the moon — armed, it turns the songs that gain by it and leaves the rest upright.
It shows up only on a device that can be turned, which is why a desktop never
sees it.

:::

## 8. Zoom into the page

The page is drawn to fit the screen it is on, which on a small screen can mean
chords you have to lean in for. So there is a zoom, and it is the app's own:

- **Pinch** with two fingers, or **scroll** with a wheel.
- **Double-tap** — or double-click — the line you want, and doing it again gives
  the whole page back.

A pill appears in the corner with the magnification on it, and it stays put while
the rest of the chrome fades. The way back out to the whole page is the pill
itself (<Ui id="zoom.reset" />).

See [Stage & audience](/stage-audience/index.mdx#zooming-in).

## 9. Leave and resume the performance

Somebody wants a song that is not in the book. Click another module in the rail
and go and get it: the performance does not end, it simply stops being on screen.
The bar goes with the page, and <Ui id="nav.stage" /> takes you straight back
into the song you left — not to the picker.

It survives more than that. Which book, which song and the state of the moon are
written to the device as you go, so a tab your phone closed while it was locked,
or a reload in the middle of a set, comes back to the same song rather than to
the top of the book. A lobby comes back with it, which is what stops an audience
being stranded by a locked phone —
[Play to an audience](./play-to-an-audience.mdx) is that whole half.

## 10. Exit

The red cross at the end of the bar, <Ui id="stage.exit" /> — on a phone, the
last row of <Ui id="stage.menu" />, in the same red. It is the only thing that
ends a performance.

Pressing it clears the lot: the book, the song you were on, the dark page, the
transpose, fullscreen, and any lobby you had open, which the audience is told
about rather than left waiting on. Then you are back at the picker in step 2,
with the evening over and the next one a fresh press of <Ui id="stage.perform" />.
